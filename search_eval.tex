\noindent The action masker allows an action only if it can certify a sequence
of actions starting with it, i.e.\ show that the sequence satisfies a COLREG
manoeuvre specification for every possible future of the obstacle. This note
explains what is checked, how the search works, which faster versions of it
were compared, and how its cost grows with depth.

%======================================================================
\section{Reachable sets, predicates and specification}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{Reachable sets.} The obstacle's future is not known, so it is
described by a \emph{reachable tube}: one ellipsoid per 40~s time step that,
with PAC confidence, contains every position and velocity the obstacle can have
at that time. Anything certified must hold for all obstacle states in the set,
not just a predicted one.

\paragraph{Predicates.} A predicate is a signed quantity of the ego and
obstacle states. Because the obstacle is a set, each predicate is evaluated as
an interval: its lowest and highest value over the reachable set. The turn
specification uses five:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep 2pt
  \item \textbf{occupancy\_clear}: distance to the obstacle beyond the safety
    radius (482.8~m, three ship lengths). Positive means no possible obstacle
    is inside the radius.
  \item \textbf{collision\_possible\_LOS}: whether the obstacle closes in
    along the line of sight fast enough to use up the remaining clearance
    within 300~s. $\le 0$ means no collision is possible at the current
    closing rate.
  \item \textbf{range\_closing}: the closing speed itself. $\le 0$ means the
    range is opening.
  \item \textbf{target\_starboard}: how far the obstacle is to starboard of
    the ego's original course line. $\le 0$ means it is not to starboard.
  \item \textbf{course\_alteration}: how much the ego's starboard course
    change exceeds the required alteration. The requirement grows smoothly to
    $20^\circ$ as the vessels close in and drops back to zero when they are
    close or past.
\end{itemize}
All except occupancy are scaled to seconds so that they can be compared.

\paragraph{Specification.} For a trace of $d$ samples (indices $0,\dots,d-1$),
\begin{align*}
  \varphi_d \;=\;& \mathbf{G}\,(\mathrm{occupancy\_clear} \ge 0)
  \;\wedge\; \mathbf{F}_{[d-2,\,d-1]}\,(\mathrm{collision\_possible\_LOS} \le 0)\\
  \wedge\;& \mathbf{G}\,\big((\mathrm{course\_alteration} \ge 0) \vee
            (\mathrm{range\_closing} \le 0) \vee
            (\mathrm{target\_starboard} \le 0)\big).
\end{align*}
In words: never enter the safety radius; by the end of the sequence the
collision risk must be resolved; and at all times, either the ego has turned
enough to starboard, or the vessels are separating, or the obstacle is no
longer to starboard (the turn rule). A sequence is \emph{certified} if the
lower bound of the robustness of $\varphi_d$ is at least $0$, i.e.\ it holds
even in the worst case over the reachable set.

%======================================================================
\section{The action search}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{Tree.} At each step the ego chooses one of five yaw-rate commands,
$\{-1, -0.5, 0, 0.5, 1\}~^\circ/\mathrm{s}$, held for 40~s. A search of depth
$d$ therefore looks $40d$~s ahead and has $5^d$ possible sequences. The
crossing-side rule (the commanded heading must not be to port of the original
course) removes about three quarters of them. Each remaining edge is one
\emph{rollout}: a 40~s closed-loop simulation of the Mariner hull and
autopilot, after which the new state is checked against the reachable set at
that time step. The search is exhaustive: every allowed sequence to depth $d$
is simulated and checked.

\paragraph{Temporal windows.} Sample $k$ is the state after $k+1$ actions,
$40(k+1)$~s after detection. The \emph{always} ($\mathbf{G}$) parts cover the
whole trace. The \emph{eventually} ($\mathbf{F}$) part only looks at the last
two samples, so it moves with the depth: a deeper search gives the ego more
time to resolve the collision risk (Table~\ref{tab:windows}).

\paragraph{Incremental evaluation.} A predicate sample depends only on the ego
state and the reachable set at its own time step. Each node therefore computes
one new sample and its children reuse it, and the formula is evaluated once
per leaf over the stored trace. Each node costs one rollout, one predicate
sample and (at the leaves) one formula evaluation.

%======================================================================
\section{Optimised versions}
%======================================================================

The versions differ only in how a node's predicate intervals are computed,
except pruning, which changes which nodes are visited. All give the same
certified sequences.

\paragraph{Production.} Most predicate bounds are the closest or farthest
point of a 2-D projection of the reachable ellipsoid to some point, e.g.\ the
minimum distance from the ego to any obstacle position. This has a closed form
up to one scalar root (the \emph{secular equation}), which production finds
with Brent's method in Python. Each predicate solves its own
problems.

\paragraph{Shared geometry.} Several predicates ask the same question: four
need the minimum and maximum ego--obstacle distance, two need the same
closing-speed bounds. This version solves each distinct problem once per
sample and shares the answer (8 root solves become 2). The results are
bit-identical.

\paragraph{Compiled roots.} On top of shared geometry, the secular equation is
solved in compiled code (numba) with Newton's method and a bisection fallback,
using the same tolerances. This avoids the roughly ten Python interpreter calls
per root that Brent's method makes. The results agree with production within tolerance.

\paragraph{Pruning (estimate).} The turn rule is an \emph{always}
($\mathbf{G}$): once it fails at one sample, no longer sequence through that
node can be certified, so the search can stop expanding it. This was not run.
Its time is estimated by removing the share of rollouts that lie below such
nodes (about 25\%).

\paragraph{Legacy method.} The legacy method computed the bounds with SLSQP over the full 6-D ellipsoid, and every node recomputed the
whole trace. It was $88\times$ slower than production at depth 7, and because
SLSQP is a local optimiser it sometimes overestimated lower bounds, which can
certify unsafe sequences.

%======================================================================
\section{Results}
%======================================================================

The search was run to depth 10 from ten detection states of a give-way
crossing (obstacle 6--10~km away), using a
reachable tube with one ellipsoid per 40~s. Times are one-core totals for an exhaustive depth-$d$ search,
median over the states. The states agree within $\pm 3\%$.

\paragraph{Growth.} From depth 5 on, the cost grows by $4.5$--$4.8\times$ per
level, following the number of rollouts, and the speed-ups stay almost
constant. A speed-up $s$ is therefore worth only $\ln s/\ln 4.8$ extra levels:
the full $2.83\times$ buys about two thirds of a level (26~s of lookahead).
With a 10-minute one-core budget, production reaches depth 8 and the optimised
search depth 9. After the optimisations, the ego rollout is half of the node
time, so reaching depth 10 requires less branching (for example longer holds
at later levels), not only faster nodes.

solve that several predicates need is paid by the first to ask. ``Alone'' is
the predicate computed without the others.

In production the collision predicate is the most expensive (a third of the
sample): it needs the minimum and maximum distance and the closing-rate
bounds. Occupancy, course alteration and range closing each need the distance
bounds too and cost 160--200~$\mu$s; target starboard is a linear bound in
closed form and costs almost nothing. Shared geometry does not make any
predicate cheaper on its own, but once occupancy and the collision predicate
have solved the distance and closing-rate problems, course alteration and
range closing reuse them almost for free. Compiled roots then make every
predicate that needs a root about $2.5$--$3\times$ cheaper. Per sample the
predicates become $4.7\times$ faster, but per search node only $2.1\times$,
because the rollout and the formula are unchanged.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Only the detection state of one crossing was
evaluated, where the tree is largest. The receding mask stops at the first
certified leaf per first action, so these times are an upper bound for it.
